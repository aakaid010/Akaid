Research

I study how to build data-driven models for engineering systems that can be trusted: validated without leakage, honest about uncertainty, and explained in ways that agree across model families.

Overview

Machine learning for industrial data is easy to make look good and hard to make reliable. Random train and test splits can leak information between records that come from the same engine, the same cutting tool, or the same paper. Feature importances can change completely from one model family to another. Point predictions rarely say how wrong they might be. My work asks what happens to accuracy, explanations, and confidence once these problems are treated as the main object of study.

My undergraduate thesis takes on validation and uncertainty for materials data. My independent work on turbofan engines takes on explanation agreement in prognostics. My current tool wear project takes on uncertainty quantification and explanation stability in high-speed CNC milling. I see these as one line of work: prognostics and predictive maintenance that an engineer can rely on.

Research Threads

1. Prognostics & remaining useful life prediction

I designed a stacking ensemble that combines Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost with a Ridge meta-learner, and applied it to RUL prediction on the NASA C-MAPSS FD001 and FD003 datasets. The pipeline covers sensor-data preprocessing, feature engineering, ensemble learning, and evaluation with Group K-Fold cross-validation, RMSE, and the NASA PHM08 scoring function. The work is described in a paper submitted to ICMIEE 2026.

2. Explainable AI & explanation agreement

Alongside accuracy, I ask whether different models explain their predictions in the same way. Using SHAP TreeExplainer, I introduced quantitative cross-model agreement metrics: Feature Agreement, Rank Agreement, Pairwise Rank Agreement, and Spearman correlation. I also carried out leakage-free SHAP-based feature pruning to examine the trade-off between predictive accuracy, feature dimensionality, and inference efficiency. In the thesis work, impurity-based and SHAP importances agreed strongly across tree ensembles (Spearman ρ > 0.88).

3. Physics-informed & uncertainty-aware machine learning

For high-speed CNC milling I am developing a multi-sensor deep-learning framework that uses cutting force, vibration, and acoustic-emission signals to predict tool wear while quantifying uncertainty. The study asks how prediction uncertainty affects explanation stability, using SHAP and Integrated Gradients, and explores risk-aware tool-replacement decisions based on calibrated predictions. On the materials side, my thesis used paper-grouped cross-validation and split-conformal prediction (MAPIE) to show what generalisation really looks like across publications, and to attach 90% prediction intervals to predictions.

Predictive Modeling of Nanocellulose Characteristics (Undergraduate Thesis)

2025 – 2026

Department of Mechanical Engineering, CUET

  • Curated a 341-record dataset across 96 publications, resolving text-string parsing errors and category imbalances in acid hydrolysis data.
  • Implemented paper-grouped cross-validation (GroupKFold), showing that conventional random splits drastically overstate generalisation (R² = 0.86 against near-zero out-of-fold accuracy).
  • Found strong Spearman rank agreement (ρ > 0.88) between impurity and SHAP feature importances across tree ensembles.
  • Applied split-conformal prediction via MAPIE to build 90% prediction intervals that quantify out-of-distribution uncertainty across publication groups.
