Research

I study how to build data-driven models for engineering systems that can be trusted: validated without leakage, honest about uncertainty, and explained in ways that agree across model families.

Overview

Machine learning for industrial data is easy to make look good and hard to make reliable. Random train and test splits can leak information between records that come from the same engine, the same cutting tool, or the same paper. Feature importances can change completely from one model family to another. Point predictions rarely say how wrong they might be. My work asks what happens to accuracy, explanations, and confidence once these problems are treated as the main object of study.

My undergraduate thesis takes on validation and uncertainty for materials data. My independent work on turbofan engines takes on explanation agreement in prognostics. My tool wear work takes on uncertainty quantification when a model is applied to a tool or a machine it has not seen. I see these as one line of work: prognostics and predictive maintenance that an engineer can rely on.

Research Threads

1. Prognostics & remaining useful life prediction

I designed a stacking ensemble that combines Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost with a Ridge meta-learner, and applied it to RUL prediction on the NASA C-MAPSS FD001 and FD003 datasets. The pipeline covers sensor-data preprocessing, feature engineering, ensemble learning, and evaluation with Group K-Fold cross-validation, RMSE, and the NASA PHM08 scoring function. The work is described in a paper accepted at ICMIEE 2026.

2. Explainable AI & explanation agreement

Alongside accuracy, I ask whether different models explain their predictions in the same way. Using SHAP TreeExplainer, I introduced quantitative cross-model agreement metrics: Feature Agreement, Rank Agreement, Pairwise Rank Agreement, and Spearman correlation. I also carried out leakage-free SHAP-based feature pruning to examine the trade-off between predictive accuracy, feature dimensionality, and inference efficiency. In the thesis work, impurity-based and SHAP importances agreed closely (Spearman ρ = 0.975 and 0.976 for the two targets).

3. Uncertainty quantification

For milling tool wear I study split-conformal prediction intervals under tool and machine shift, on 12 tools and 4 machines from two public datasets. The question is where the calibration residuals should come from. On a new tool, residuals from interleaved blocks of the training tools covered only 53.7% at nominal 90%, while leave-one-tool-out calibration covered 88.8%; on a new machine, leave-one-machine-out calibration was needed to restore coverage. A manuscript has been submitted. On the materials side, my thesis used paper-grouped cross-validation and split-conformal prediction to attach 90% prediction intervals to predictions across publications.

Current Work

Calibration Sources for Tool-Wear Prediction Intervals Under Tool and Machine Shift in Milling

Submitted

Independent research · journal manuscript · Code on GitHub

  • Compared five sources of calibration residuals for split-conformal wear intervals, with the point model (Random Forest and gradient boosting) held fixed, on 12 tools and 4 machines from two public milling datasets. The nine-tool protocol was fixed in advance.
  • On a new tool, residuals from interleaved blocks of the training tools covered 53.7% at nominal 90% (worst tool 13.0%); leave-one-tool-out calibration covered 88.8% with the lowest interval score, at about one-third the width of late-life calibration.
  • On a new machine, leave-one-tool-out coverage fell to 65.8%; leave-one-machine-out calibration raised it to 94.5% at 1.9× the width.

Predictive Modeling of Nanocellulose Characteristics (Undergraduate Thesis)

2025 – 2026

Department of Mechanical Engineering, CUET · Supervisor: Dr. M. S. Rabbi · paper accepted at ICMIME 2026

  • Compiled 341 observations from 96 publications linking acid concentration, temperature, time, and feedstock to cellulose nanocrystal (CNC) crystallinity and length, and compared seven regressors with paper-grouped splits and cross-validation.
  • Random-split R² of 0.71 (crystallinity) and 0.86 (length) fell to negative (best −0.30) and near zero (best 0.05) under paper-grouped evaluation, reported as protocol sensitivity.
  • SHAP and impurity rankings agreed (Spearman 0.975 and 0.976). 90% conformal intervals reached 100% and 95.8% coverage but were wide.
