Independent Researcher: Tool-Wear Prediction & Uncertainty Quantification

2026

Journal manuscript submitted to The International Journal of Advanced Manufacturing Technology · PHM2010 and nine-tool milling datasets

  • Compared five sources of calibration residuals for split-conformal wear intervals with the point model held fixed (Random Forest and gradient boosting), on 12 tools and 4 machines from two public datasets; the nine-tool protocol was fixed beforehand.
  • New tool (nine-tool data, Random Forest, nominal 90%): residuals from interleaved blocks of the training tools covered 53.7% (worst tool 13.0%). Leave-one-tool-out calibration covered 88.8% with the lowest interval score, beating late-life calibration on all 9 tools (p = 0.004, both models) at about one-third the width.
  • New machine: leave-one-tool-out coverage fell to 65.8% (worst 26.1%); leave-one-machine-out calibration raised coverage on all 9 tools, to 94.5% (worst 74.1%), at 1.9× the width.
  • PHM2010 (interleaved split as a deliberately optimistic case): the same collapse, 16.8% coverage with same-cutter residuals against 84.3% with another cutter’s. Random Forest beat a sensor-free cut-index curve on every cutter (MAE 14.5 against 21.1 µm interleaved; 22.7 against 26.4 µm chronological), and its tuned first warnings had lower decision cost (66 against 303; 154 against 742).
  • Code (release v1.1, archived on Zenodo): github.com/aakaid010/phm2010-tool-wear-reliability.

Independent Researcher: Prognostics & Explainable AI

2025 – 2026

NASA C-MAPSS FD001/FD003 · with N. I. Rafi · conference paper accepted at ICMIEE 2026

  • Built a leakage-aware pipeline stacking Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost with a Ridge meta-learner, using engine-grouped cross-validation. The best single learners reached test RMSE 17.57 (FD001) and 19.39 (FD003), and stacking did not beat them.
  • Introduced cross-model SHAP explanation-agreement metrics (feature, rank, and pairwise-rank agreement, and Spearman correlation); mean pairwise-rank agreement of 0.87 (FD001) and 0.84 (FD003) explained why stacking added little.
  • Leakage-free SHAP pruning with nested GroupKFold: on FD001, 5 of 51 features gave RMSE 17.30 against 17.57 for the full set; FD003 needed about 30 features.
  • Code: github.com/aakaid010/RUL-Prediction-using-Nasa-C-MAPSS-DATA.

Undergraduate Thesis Researcher: ML for Nanocellulose Properties

2025 – 2026

Department of Mechanical Engineering, CUET · Supervisor: Dr. M. S. Rabbi · conference paper accepted at ICMIME 2026

  • Compiled 341 observations from 96 publications linking acid concentration, temperature, time, and feedstock to cellulose nanocrystal (CNC) crystallinity and length; compared seven regressors with paper-grouped splits and cross-validation.
  • Random-split R² of 0.71 (crystallinity) and 0.86 (length) fell to negative (best −0.30) and near zero (best 0.05) under paper-grouped evaluation (reported as protocol sensitivity).
  • SHAP and impurity rankings agreed (Spearman 0.975 and 0.976). 90% conformal intervals reached 100% and 95.8% coverage but were wide.
  • Code: github.com/aakaid010/NanoCNC-Predictor.
